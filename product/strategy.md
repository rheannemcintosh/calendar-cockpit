# Product Strategy

This document sets out how Calendar Cockpit will move towards its vision: who it is for first, how it will be different from the calendars people already use, and where effort will go and in what order. It builds on the problem statement and personas, and is the reference for deciding what to build next and what to leave out.

## The Vision

A world where people can plan their time in the right-sized pieces and see at a glance what each one is and which part of life it belongs to, so their calendar reflects how they actually spend their day.

## Where We'll Play

**Personal life outside work.** Calendar Cockpit is for the calendar a person keeps for everything outside their job: health, deliveries and errands, exercise, family and friends, study, and side projects. Their working day stays in the calendar their employer runs. The product doesn't try to replace it, schedule meetings or show availability to colleagues.

**The System Builder first.** The first users are people who already plan their life in a calendar and have built their own system of colours and emojis to make up for what it lacks. They feel the problem most sharply, know exactly what they need, and will notice straight away if the product doesn't fit how they think. Building for them first proves the core ideas against the most demanding user.

**The Head Planner next.** Once the core works for the System Builder, the product widens to people who keep their soft plans out of a calendar altogether. They need the same core, plus a faster way in: quick entry and a starting set of categories they can adjust later.

## How We'll Win

Calendars treat every event as the same kind of thing and describe it in terms of availability. Calendar Cockpit wins by describing each piece of time on its own terms, so the calendar itself understands what the System Builder currently tracks by hand.

- **Time types are built in, not bolted on.** Every item says how precise its time is: exact, a window, a protected block, or approximate. This is the one thing no mainstream calendar offers, and it is what lets a person tell at a glance what can move and what must stay put.
- **Categories have names and depth.** Each category and sub-category has its own name, colour and emoji, so a colour always means something and a physio appointment can be told apart from a GP appointment within health. This replaces both the remembered colour code and the emoji typed into each event name.
- **The kind of commitment is recorded.** Whether something is pre-paid, can't be cancelled, is important, is tentative or is only an idea is stored against the item rather than kept in the person's head or a second emoji.
- **It fits the person's model.** Categories and sub-categories are the user's own, not a fixed set, and a sub-category can carry a default time type so the right one is picked without thinking.
- **It reads at a glance.** All of this comes together in a view where the shape, colour and label of each item show what it is, which part of life it belongs to and how firm it is, without opening it.

## Where We'll Focus

The work is ordered so that each step makes the product more useful to the System Builder before effort moves to widening it.

1. **Model time honestly.** Calendar items with a time type, so exact times, windows, blocks and approximate times can all be recorded truthfully. This is the foundation everything else rests on.
2. **Make categories mean something.** Named categories and sub-categories with colours, emojis and a default time type, shown on each item.
3. **See the day at a glance.** A calendar view that makes the time type, category and sub-category of each item readable without opening it, so a day's real shape and spare room are clear.
4. **Record the kind of commitment.** Mark items as pre-paid, non-cancellable, important, tentative or an idea, and show it in the view, so rearranging a day is a matter of looking rather than remembering.
5. **Lower the way in.** Quick entry for a rough time or a window, and a sensible starting set of categories, so the Head Planner can get value from the first item.
6. **Fit alongside existing calendars.** Ways to bring in items from, or show them next to, the calendars people already use, so moving across doesn't mean starting from nothing.

## What We Won't Do

- **Work productivity.** No meeting scheduling, team availability, focus-time analytics or task management for work.
- **Automatic scheduling.** The product won't move items or fill free slots on the person's behalf. It shows what can move; the person decides.
- **A fixed category system.** There is no one right set of categories. A starting set is offered as a starting point, not a rule.
- **More colours as the answer.** Colour on its own was never the fix. Every colour belongs to a named category.

## How We'll Know It's Working

- **Flexible items get recorded.** A healthy share of items use a time type other than exact, showing that windows, blocks and rough plans are going into the calendar instead of being left out.
- **Items are categorised.** Most items have a sub-category, showing that the category system is being used instead of names typed with emojis.
- **The hand-built system is retired.** The System Builder stops typing emojis into item names to mark sub-categories and commitment kinds.
- **Getting started is quick.** A new user adds their first useful items in their first session, without having to design a category system first.
